Folk Poetry/Music: Negative Metaphor

A negative metaphor is a metaphor that describes something by saying what it is not, rather than directly saying what it is.

For example: "This family conflict is no ordinary war.” Here, “war” is treated metaphorically as something that can be “ordinary.” By saying it is no ordinary war, the speaker emphasizes that the conflict is exceptional or qualitatively different.

A negative metaphor can be especially useful when a writer rejects an established metaphor:
“Ukraine is not Russia’s backyard.” The phrase invokes the metaphor of a “backyard”—a space presumed to belong to or be controlled by someone—but negates it. The metaphor therefore does two things at once: it evokes a familiar way of imagining a geopolitical relationship and challenges that way of imagining it.

Now to Russian folk poetry.

This is important because Russian folklore frequently creates a parallel between nature and human emotion: Nature → apparent explanation → negation → human/emotional reality

For example:
Не ветер во поле шумит
-то тоскует сердце моё.

It is not the wind that is rustling in the field
-it is my heart that is longing.

Here the negative metaphor redirects the listener from the physical world to the emotional world.
