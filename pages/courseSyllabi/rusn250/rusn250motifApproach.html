Motif Approach

The motif approach to folklore is a method of analyzing traditional narratives by breaking them down into their smallest recurring, striking narrative elements—known as motifs—and comparing them across different cultures and eras. Scholars Antti Aarne in 1904, Stith Thompson, and Hans-Jörg Uther in 2004 developed this approach.

Tale Types and Indexes

Created across six volumes by folklorist Stith Thompson, the Motif-Index organizes narrative building blocks into 26 broad alphabetical classes (A through Z)—ranging from Mythological Motifs (A) and Magic (D) to Deceptions (K) and Humor (X).

This is useful for getting a visual sense of the organization:
1–299: Animal Tales
300–749: Tales of Magic
750–849: Religious Tales
850–999: Realistic Tales
1000–1199: Tales of the Stupid Ogre
1200–1999: Anecdotes and Jokes
2000–2399: Formula Tales

The one distinction you should learn first
The easiest way to understand ATU is to distinguish tale type from motif:
Tale type = the basic plot
Cinderella → ATU 510A
Motif = a smaller recurring element

Motifs can include specific characters (ogres, helpful animals), actions/incidents (getting three wishes, magical transformations), objects (magic rings, poisoned apples), magical transformation, or settings..
