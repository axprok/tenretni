Fourth Wave

The fourth wave of emigration from the USSR (and the post-Soviet space) began in the late 1980s through the early 1990s, coinciding with Mikhail Gorbachev’s policies of glasnost and perestroika, the fall of the Berlin Wall, and the final dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991.

    Key Characteristics of the Fourth Wave
  • Timeframe: Roughly 1987–1991 into the post-Soviet 1990s.
  • Primary Motivations: Economic crisis, the lifting of Soviet exit bans, political instability, and family reunification.
  • Shift to General Migration: From the mid-to-late 1990s onward, the demographic broadened from targeted ethnic/refugee flows into a steady, functioning economic migration of workers, businesspeople, and professionals of various nationalities, including ethnic Russians.
  • Destinations: Major destination countries included the United States (such as New York's Brighton Beach or Chicago), Israel, Germany, and Canada.
