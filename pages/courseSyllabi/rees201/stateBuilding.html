State Building in Russia after the USSR

State-building is the structural and institutional project of developing formal governmental capacity, administrative systems, and coercive authority.

Nation-building is the cultural and psychological project of fostering a shared collective identity, national unity, and civic loyalty among a population (often through education, common languages, and national symbols)

Key Elements of State building.
1 Monopoly on Violence: Claiming legitimate control over instruments of force and security, typically via a standing military and police force.

2 Rule of Law and Legal Frameworks: Developing formal judicial systems, standard legal procedures, and legislative bodies to make and interpret laws.

According to Stephen Hanson, Russia's post-Soviet state-building process was marked by the collapse of state institutions under Yeltsin, when privatization, corruption, and powerful regional actors weakened the government's authority. Putin subsequently recentralized political power, subordinated oligarchs and regional governors, and strengthened presidential control, but relied on personal loyalty and informal networks rather than independent institutions. Hanson argues that Putin created a patrimonial state: politically powerful and highly centralized, yet institutionally weak, corrupt, and unable to govern effectively through impartial laws and professional bureaucracy.
