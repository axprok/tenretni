Second Wave

The second wave of Russian emigration took place during and immediately after World War II (the 1940s–1950s).

Key Characteristics
Who left: It comprised Soviet citizens who found themselves outside the USSR or were displaced during the war, including:
Soviet prisoners of war (POWs)
Ostarbeiter (forced laborers taken to Germany)
Refugees and civilian deportees
Members of military groups like the Russian Liberation Army (ROA) / Vlasov movement (fought on German side against the Red Army).
Post-war escapees and political defectors

Motivations: Many refused to return to the Soviet Union after the war—some out of fear of Stalinist repression and Gulag imprisonment for being captured or living under German occupation, and others because they rejected the Soviet system. • Numbers: Official Soviet figures minimized the scale (around 130,000), but modern historians and researchers estimate the actual number of "nonreturners" and displaced persons ranged from 500,000 to 700,000 (with broader wartime displacement totals in the millions). • Relocation and Camps: A large portion initially lived in Allied-administered Displaced Persons (DP) camps in the Western zones of Germany, Austria, and Italy before eventually resettling globally—including in the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, and South America. Munich became a major intellectual and cultural hub for this wave
