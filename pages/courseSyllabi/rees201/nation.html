What does Anderson mean by nation as an "imagined community"?

Anderson argues that a nation is an imagined political community because:
Most members of a nation will never meet or personally know one another.
Nevertheless, they imagine themselves as belonging to the same community.
They share a sense of common identity, history, culture, and political destiny.
This collective belonging creates solidarity among people who are otherwise strangers. Importantly, imagined does not mean fake or unreal. It means that national belonging is constructed through shared ideas, symbols, narratives, and institutions.

How is a nation created? Anderson emphasizes several mechanisms:
Print capitalism: Newspapers and novels allow people to imagine that they belong to the same community.
Common language: Standardized languages create a shared cultural space.
Shared historical narratives: People come to understand themselves as having a common past.
National institutions: Schools, museums, maps, censuses, and state institutions reinforce national identity.
