Film Form

In his approach to film analysis, Timothy Corrigan understands film form as the organized system of cinematic choices through which a film produces meaning. Five fundamental elements constitute film form: mise-en-scène, cinematography, editing, sound, and narrative.

Mise-en-scène encompasses everything placed before the camera, including setting, lighting, costume, makeup, props, and acting.

Cinematography concerns how images are captured and presented through camera placement, framing, camera movement, focus, and composition. Editing determines how individual shots are arranged and connected, establishing the film's rhythm, continuity, and relationships between images. Sound includes dialogue, music, sound effects, and silence, shaping the atmosphere and emotional experience of a film. Narrative refers to the organization of events, characters, time, and point of view through which a story unfolds. These five elements do not operate independently; rather, they interact to shape the spectator's experience and construct cinematic meaning. Analyzing film form therefore means examining not simply what a film represents but how it represents it.
