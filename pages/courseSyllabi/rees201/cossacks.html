Cossacks & Chumaks (my mom's maiden last name is Chumachenko)

  • The word is of Turkic origins. Means "a free man"
  • Russian and Ukrainian words "cossack" and etnonym "Kazakh" are related
  • Cossacks in Ukraine is an early modern state, a warrior democracy that emerged in the 1500s. They resided along the Dnieper river
  • 1648: Under Bohdan Khmelnytsky, the Cossacks led a major uprising against Polish-Lithuanian rule. This marked a decisive turning point in Ukrainian history and the emergence of the Cossack Hetmanate.
  • Russian cossacks lived on the Don river, Ural/Yaik river, Amur river
  • Cossacks formed a state community/quasi state called "hosts." The most famous host is the Zaporizhian Sich (emerged around 1552).
  • Location: cossacks resided/reside on frontiers
  • Economy: cossacks sustained themselves via multiple trades (fished, hunted, even farmed, & served as soldiers for political authorities) but raid economy was one of their distinct features as a community. A raid economy is a system in which a community obtains a significant portion of its wealth through organized raids on other communities, trade routes,rather than primarily through agriculture, or manufacturing.
    See also vikings, pirates of the Carribean, pirates in Somali territorial waters
  • Economy: Ukrainian and Don river Cossacks attacked Ottoman and Crimean Tatar coastal settlements, fortresses, and ships, seizing goods and other valuables. (1) Captives and ransom: Captured people could be ransomed or sold into slavery, although Cossacks also fought to free Christian captives held in Ottoman and Crimean Tatar territories. (2) Maritime expeditions (piracy): Cossacks used swift, shallow-draft boats called chaiky (seagulls) to attack Ottoman targets along the Black Sea coast.
  • Economy: Because many of cossack raids happened in steppe (an Eastern European counterpart of North American prairie), horse breading was a common and respectable economic practice amond cossacks.
  • Economy: salt trade. Salt was an expensive commodity in early modern Europe. Cossacks harvested salt, controlled salk trade in Southern Ukraine and Russia; they heavily interacted with the Chumaks, a famous class of independent Ukrainian merchant-transporters (who specialized in salt trade).
  • An Inclusive Class: Chumak society bypassed traditional feudal barriers. Anyone who could afford an ox and a cart could join—ranging from wealthy peasants and townspeople to Cossacks and even serfs working to pay off their taxes. The trade was so lucrative that successful Chumak families often stopped farming entirely, relying solely on trade profits. Chumaks cooperated with cossacks (who provided armed protection) on running trade roots on the rivers.

    The Chumaks impact on Ukrainian culture is literally written in the stars: in Ukraine, the Milky Way galaxy is traditionally called "The Chumak Way." Folklore says the galaxy was formed when traveling Chumaks accidentally spilled a trail of white salt across the night sky as a glowing roadmap to guide them home
  • Identity. The frontier life and raid economy generated a certain type of distinct cossack identity. Cossacks were known for their willingness to take risk during their expeditions, even losing life during those raids. Uncertain outcomes and High rewards. A successful raid could bring substantial wealth, while defeat could mean death, enslavement, or capture. Resolving conflicts with arms was always a strong possibility
