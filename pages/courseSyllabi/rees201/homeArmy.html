The Polish Home Army (Armia Krajowa, AK) and the People's Army (Armia Ludowa, AL) were two major Polish underground military organizations that fought against Nazi Germany during World War II. However, they differed significantly in their political orientation, leadership, relationship with the Soviet Union, and vision for postwar Poland. 1. The Home Army vs. The People's Army in Poland

The Home Army (Armiia Krajowa, AK)
Established: February 1942 (successor to the Union of Armed Struggle).
Political orientation: Polish nationalism, anti-communism, and loyalty to the Polish government-in-exile in London.
Leadership: General Stefan Rowecki (until 1943), followed by General Tadeusz Bór-Komorowski.
Main objective: Liberate Poland from Nazi occupation and restore an independent Polish state.
Major action: The Warsaw Uprising of August–October 1944.

The Home Army was the largest Polish resistance organization. It recognized the authority of the Polish government-in-exile in London and sought to restore Poland's sovereignty after the war. Although it fought Nazi Germany, it was also deeply suspicious of Soviet intentions because Stalin sought political control over postwar Poland.

2. The People's Army (Armia Ludowa, AL)
Established: January 1944.
Political orientation: Communist and pro-Soviet.
Leadership: Michał Żymierski was its commander-in-chief; the organization was associated with the Polish Workers' Party (PPR).
Main objective: Fight Nazi Germany while promoting a communist-led political transformation of Poland.
Political alignment: Supported by the Soviet Union.

The People's Army emerged from the communist underground organization People's Guard (Gwardia Ludowa). It was connected to the Polish Workers' Party, which sought to establish a socialist state in Poland.
